Strategy

Low Cost LeadershipWalmart already dominates in low-cost leadership, being one of the lowest-cost producers.
Customer IntimacyWalmart must use technology to improve online customer relationships and loyalty, the way Amazon has.

Value Chain

Support activities

  • Firm Infrastructure: use a business management software that tracks everything
  • Human Resource Management: needs to improve — use upscaled digital programs like UC's SHAPE
  • Technology Development: invest in computer systems, AI, and/or e-commerce, like Amazon
  • Procurement: use digital procurement and supply management software to improve efficiency

Primary activities

  • Inbound Logistics: study UPS tracking systems that track goods in real time, help predict demand, and optimize workload without the need for human intervention
  • Operations: expand into cloud-based management — no need for physical servers, maybe consider automation
  • Outbound Logistics: collaborate with companies that have already perfected route optimization, potentially internationally
  • Marketing & Sales: increase data analytics for targeted ads and promotions for specific crowds
  • Service: consider AI assistants — for example, DoorDash has automated service that works well, and if it fails, brings you to an agent

Vision, Mission, and Values

Vision

Help people shop easier and more affordably all around the world.

Mission

Use Walmart's funds to improve its online scale and technology, building fast, cheap, and easy-to-use online shopping.

Values

  • Put customers' and employees' needs first
  • Use technology to make operations easier
  • Keep prices low
  • Always try to improve and be responsible

Goals

Better Website

  • Make the online store faster and easier to use — improve UI, since the current site is similar to Amazon's but could be more distinctive and smoother on the eyes
  • Measure related KPIs (e.g. visitors, average session duration, top visited pages)

Stronger Brand Recognition

  • Improve social media, with a focus on feedback and reviews
  • Build relevance with the next generation as they enter adulthood
  • Improve employee loyalty and HR practices — many younger shoppers, especially in blue states, are aware of Walmart's labor practices and have chosen not to shop there as a result. Fixing this could meaningfully improve brand perception.
"Some 40 lawsuits accuse WM of a failure to pay overtime. Accusations have also been made of widespread sex discrimination to keep a class of employees — women — at lower wages." (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics, 2004)

Increased Efficiency in Logistics

  • Deliver packages faster with fewer or no mistakes
  • Measure current delivery times, shipping costs, and routes

Initiatives

Goal A — Improved WebsiteOnboard software engineering and UI/UX employees; upgrade the site with AI assistant features; connect store and online inventory; study Amazon and Target for what to keep and what to improve.
Goal B — Make Walmart Feel ModernCreate a fresh design for the website and social media; work with eco-friendly brands to attract younger shoppers; improve labor ethics to build trust with the newer generation.
Goal C — More Efficient LogisticsUse AI or collaborate with software like Waze to build better routes, similar to UPS and Amazon; build delivery centers near stores; increase international logistics operations with an AI-driven ERP.
Goal D — Keep Customers FrequentIncrease Walmart+ benefits, including digitally; use data analytics to remind specific customers about deals and restocks.

Tactics

  • Check the website and fix slow pages (basically everything)
  • Add product recommendations using visitor statistics
  • Bring in good design and marketing professionals
  • Test faster delivery in 3 local cities, to limit the test without risking a larger failure
  • Use AI assistant bots to improve customer service
  • Measure progress monthly and fix weak or stagnant areas

Summary

Walmart should renovate itself for the new generation — increasing marketing, e-commerce, logistics, and labor ethics. Walmart should consider reaching out to outside companies or former employees to better understand what strong brand recognition and logistics actually look like.

UPS has made supply chain management a real competitive advantage: it commands 20–30% of U.S. shipping volume while generating 35% of the industry's revenue, showing how efficient and valuable its services are (Red Stag Fulfillment, 2025). Logistics can help a company stay ahead of the competition — Walmart should pursue this to compete with Amazon.

Logistics and e-commerce aren't the only things Walmart needs to work on — marketing and brand recognition matter too. The upcoming generation is increasingly aware of Walmart's history of labor practices through platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and X; stories about low wages, poor working conditions, and limited benefits spread quickly and shape public opinion. Some consumers have chosen to reduce their shopping at Walmart as a result, favoring businesses they believe treat employees better. This generation values ethical business practices, social responsibility, and transparency.

According to the Markkula Center for Applied Ethics

  • The average worker earns $8/hour for a 32-hour week — about $13,312/year, below the poverty line
  • Two-thirds of workers can't afford Walmart's health insurance, which takes 20% of their paycheck
  • Wages drop around 5% in areas where a new Walmart opens
  • 56% of Americans believe Walmart is bad for the country

To rebuild trust with the newer generation, Walmart should:

  • Raise wages to meet regional living standards
  • Provide affordable healthcare options for all employees
  • Improve workplace conditions
  • Invest in marketing that shows genuine change and progress
